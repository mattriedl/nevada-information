There are six Nevada ski resorts and ski areas you can visit: Mt. Rose and Diamond Peak near Reno and Lake Tahoe, the Nevada side of Heavenly at Stateline, Lee Canyon less than an hour from Las Vegas, and two small community hills, Sky Tavern and the Elko SnoBowl. This guide compares them on terrain, prices, opening dates and driving, and explains Nevada's chain rules so you get there safely.

Quick answer

  • Closest to Reno: Mt. Rose Ski Tahoe, about 25 miles from downtown, has Tahoe's highest base at 8,260 feet. It opened Nov. 6 this season.
  • Best lake views: Diamond Peak above Incline Village plans to open Dec. 3 for its 60th season. Online adult tickets start at $149.
  • Biggest: Heavenly straddles the state line with 4,800 acres. Its two Nevada base lodges in Stateline have free parking.
  • Near Las Vegas: Lee Canyon on Mount Charleston opens Nov. 20, and a new Wild West Bowl chairlift is expected Dec. 12.
  • Before you drive: check nvroads.com or call 511. On the Mt. Rose Highway, cars without chains must be 4WD or AWD with snow tires during chain controls.

Does Nevada have ski resorts?

Yes. Nevada has four lift-served public resorts plus two volunteer-run ski areas, and the snow is better than most people expect from a desert state. The best skiing is in the Carson Range on the Nevada side of Lake Tahoe, where Mt. Rose averages about 350 inches of snow a winter. In southern Nevada, Lee Canyon sits high in the Spring Mountains, so Las Vegas residents can ski in the morning and be back on the Strip for dinner.

Skiing in Nevada splits into three areas. Around Reno and the north shore of Lake Tahoe you'll find Mt. Rose, Diamond Peak and Sky Tavern. At the south end of the lake, Heavenly's Nevada base lodges are in Stateline. Lee Canyon serves Las Vegas, and the Elko SnoBowl serves northeastern Nevada. For more on the north, see our Reno-Tahoe region guide.

All prices and dates below come from the resorts' own websites in November. Lift ticket prices at the big resorts change with demand, so treat them as a starting point and check before you go.

Mt. Rose Ski Tahoe: the closest ski resort to Reno

Mt. Rose is the go-to ski resort near Reno, with the highest base elevation at Lake Tahoe and some of the steepest terrain in the region. It sits at the summit of the Mt. Rose Highway (State Route 431), between Reno and Incline Village. The resort lists the drive as about 25 minutes from the Reno-Tahoe airport and 10 minutes from Incline Village.

The mountain's numbers, from Mt. Rose: base 8,260 feet, summit 9,700 feet, 1,800 vertical feet, more than 1,200 acres and 70-plus trails, with a 2.5-mile longest run. The terrain is 20% beginner, 30% intermediate, 40% advanced and 10% expert. That expert slice includes the Chutes, more than 200 acres of steep, gated terrain with pitches up to 55 degrees, reached from the Northwest Express and Zephyr Express chairs.

Mt. Rose opened on Nov. 6 this season, two weeks ahead of its original target. After a storm dropped 32 to 36 inches, nearly half the mountain was open by Nov. 19, with the Northwest Express running to the summit and all beginner terrain open, SnowBrains reported. The Galena beginner chair is scheduled for Nov. 25, and the Wildslide snow tubing park opens on Thanksgiving Day, Nov. 26.

Mt. Rose lift tickets

Mt. Rose uses dynamic pricing. Full window prices are $189 for adults (16+) and $95 for kids (6-15), and tickets for kids 5 and under are $20. You'll pay less by buying online by midnight the day before, using the resort's pricing calendar. Half-day tickets run 9 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. or 12:30 to 4 p.m. Weekday deals include Two'fer Tuesdays, Thursday Ladies' Day and Locals Sundays on non-holidays. Lifts run 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. The resort doesn't run a shuttle from Reno, so plan to drive.

Diamond Peak: Lake Tahoe views from Incline Village

Diamond Peak is a smaller, community-owned resort above Incline Village, known for its views straight down to Lake Tahoe and for being easy to manage with kids. It opened as Ski Incline on Nov. 19, 1966, so this is its 60th season. The projected opening day is Dec. 3, conditions permitting, and it typically runs to mid-April.

The mountain runs from 6,700 to 8,540 feet, with 1,840 vertical feet, 655 acres and 30 runs, plus glades and tree skiing. The mix is 18% beginner, 46% intermediate and 36% advanced, with a 2.1-mile longest run. Snowmaking covers 75% of the developed terrain, according to Diamond Peak.

Diamond Peak lift tickets

Diamond Peak sets three price levels: Value (weekdays), Weekend and Peak (holidays). Online adult tickets (ages 24-64) are $149, $179 and $199. Youth (13-23) and seniors (65-79) pay $129, $159 and $179, and kids 7-12 pay $60, $79 and $99. Kids 6 and under and skiers 80 and over ski free. Window tickets cost $10 more, and there are no half-day tickets. This season's peak periods are Dec. 19 to Jan. 3, Jan. 16-18 and Feb. 13-21.

For the anniversary, single-day tickets bought online are $60 on five "Deal Days": opening day, Jan. 12, Feb. 8, March 17 and closing day, per the resort's 60th anniversary announcement. Chairlifts run 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. daily.

Heavenly: skiing two states from Stateline

Heavenly is the biggest resort at Lake Tahoe, and it's the one where you can ski from Nevada into California and back. It covers 4,800 acres with 28 lifts and 111 trails, from a base of 6,657 feet to a 10,067-foot summit, the highest at Lake Tahoe, according to Heavenly. It averages 251 inches of snow, and the terrain is 15% beginner, 53% intermediate and 32% advanced.

Heavenly has four base lodges, two in each state. The Nevada ones are Stagecoach Lodge (375 Quaking Aspen Lane) and Boulder Lodge (140 S. Benjamin Drive), both in Stateline. Parking at both stays free every day this season. At the California Lodge, reservations are needed on weekends and peak days starting Dec. 12, with a fee for cars carrying three or fewer people before 11:30 a.m. So the Nevada lodges are the easier choice on busy days.

Heavenly's projected opening day is Nov. 20. It's a Vail Resorts mountain, so the Epic Pass and Tahoe Local passes work here, and day tickets are cheapest when bought online ahead of time. From Reno, Stateline is roughly an hour and a half by car in good weather, via U.S. 395 and either Kingsbury Grade (State Route 207) or U.S. 50. Both of those mountain roads have strict winter tire rules (see below). Read more about the area on our Stateline page.

Lee Canyon: skiing near Las Vegas

Lee Canyon is the only ski resort near Las Vegas, about 45 minutes northwest of the city on Mount Charleston. The resort says it is "just under an hour" from the Strip. Its base lodge sits at 8,660 feet in the Spring Mountains, part of the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest, where it runs under a Forest Service permit. That elevation is why there's real snow just 45 minutes from the desert floor.

This winter starts Friday, Nov. 20. The resort has 445 acres: 235 lift-served and 210 reached by hiking. It lists 36 trails, four chairlifts (Sherwood, Bluebird, Ponderosa and Rabbit Peak) and 860 vertical feet of lift-served skiing, per its mountain statistics. The big news this season is the Wild West Bowl chairlift, reached from the Bluebird chair. It adds six intermediate and advanced trails and raises lift-served vertical to 1,000 feet, and it's expected to open Dec. 12, conditions permitting, News 3 Las Vegas reported. New beginner terrain with a rope tow and a magic carpet is part of the same project.

Lee Canyon lift tickets

Lee Canyon lift tickets must be bought online at leecanyonlv.com. Prices go up with demand and can rise quickly around storms and holidays. The lowest dates start at $9 plus $10 in "Lee Cash" for food and gear, so buying early pays off. Parking is free. Lee Canyon is on the Power Pass, and kids 12 and under ski free with a Power Kids pass. Tubes and sleds aren't allowed on the resort's slopes, so families who want to sled should look for designated snow play areas in the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest instead.

Lee Canyon Road (State Route 156) climbs from U.S. 95. There's no cell service on the way up, and traffic is heavy on weekends and holidays, so leave early and check chain controls first. Sky Tavern and the Elko SnoBowl: Nevada's small ski hills

Nevada's two small ski areas are run for locals, and both are among the cheapest ways to learn to ski in the West. They're not destination resorts, but they matter if you live nearby or are moving to the area.

Sky Tavern (Reno)

Sky Tavern, on the Mt. Rose Highway outside Reno, calls itself America's oldest nonprofit ski and snowboard learning center, running since 1948. Its Learn to Ski & Ride program usually starts in mid-January and runs on Saturdays and Sundays for eight weeks. It's a members-only family program: everyone needs a membership, and adult members volunteer for two hours each day they take part. Memberships start at $35 per weekend, the Tahoe Daily Tribune reported. Its annual Ski Swap for used gear is Nov. 20-21 at 4813 Kietzke Lane in Reno, according to Sky Tavern.

Elko SnoBowl (Elko)

The Elko SnoBowl, six miles north of Elko, is the only ski area in eastern Nevada. Volunteers run it on weekends from January to mid-March, weather permitting. It has a double chairlift added in 1998 and a free rope tow, with 700 vertical feet between a 6,300-foot base and a 7,000-foot top. Half the runs are beginner and half intermediate, and there's tubing and sledding too. Lift tickets are very cheap and sold at the Elko Chamber of Commerce, according to Travel Nevada.

Where can you heli-ski in Nevada?

For deep backcountry powder, Ruby Mountain Heli-Experience flies skiers into the Ruby Mountains southeast of Elko. Run by the Royer family, it calls itself the oldest family-run heli-ski operation in the country. Guests stay at the Ruby 360 Lodge and ski about 200,000 acres of wilderness below peaks such as 11,387-foot Ruby Dome. Three-day packages guarantee 15-18 runs, and snowcat skiing is the backup on no-fly days, per Travel Nevada. Call 775-753-6867 for prices and dates. The closest town page is Lamoille, at the mouth of Lamoille Canyon.

What are Nevada's chain and snow tire rules?

Nevada has two chain control levels, and the Tahoe-area highways to the ski resorts have a stricter rule than the rest of the state. According to the Nevada Department of Transportation:

  • Chains or snow tires required: vehicles under 10,000 pounds need snow tires or approved traction devices. Four-wheel drive is OK with four mud-and-snow (M+S) tires.
  • Chains required, except 4WD with snow tires: every vehicle needs chains unless it's four-wheel drive with four M+S tires.
  • Tahoe highways: on the Mt. Rose Highway (SR 431), Kingsbury Grade (SR 207) and U.S. 50 between Glenbrook and Carson City, any vehicle without chains during chain controls must be 4WD or AWD with snow tires.
  • What counts as a snow tire: at least 3/16 inch of tread and an M+S, M-S or M/S marking on the sidewall.

Lee Canyon advises carrying chains even in a 4WD or AWD vehicle, because conditions on the mountain change fast. Check NVRoads or call 511 before you leave, carry water, warm layers and a full tank, and expect the Mt. Rose Highway to close for a while during heavy storms. Rental cars often come with all-season tires, so ask the counter what the tires are rated for before you head up.

Tips for skiing in Nevada

Buy tickets online, go on a weekday if you can, and start early. A few habits help at every Nevada resort:

  • Book ahead. Mt. Rose, Diamond Peak, Heavenly and Lee Canyon all charge less online, and Lee Canyon sells tickets only online.
  • Avoid the holiday crush. Prices and crowds peak from about Dec. 19 to Jan. 3 and on the Martin Luther King and Presidents' Day weekends.
  • Respect the altitude. Every base on this list is above 6,000 feet, and Lee Canyon and Mt. Rose are above 8,000. Drink plenty of water and wear sunscreen, since the sun is strong at high elevation even in January.
  • Pair it with a soak. After a cold day on the slopes near Reno or Carson City, a hot spring is close by. See our Nevada hot springs guide.
  • Stay for the holidays. December in Reno is busy with lights and events; see Christmas in Reno.

Nevada ski resorts FAQ

How many ski resorts are in Nevada?

Nevada has four lift-served public resorts: Mt. Rose Ski Tahoe, Diamond Peak, Lee Canyon and the Nevada side of Heavenly. It also has two volunteer or nonprofit ski areas, Sky Tavern near Reno and the Elko SnoBowl, plus heli-skiing in the Ruby Mountains.

What is the closest ski resort to Reno?

Mt. Rose Ski Tahoe is the closest full ski resort to Reno, about 25 miles from downtown on the Mt. Rose Highway (SR 431). Sky Tavern, a nonprofit learn-to-ski hill on the same highway, is open only to its members.

Is there skiing near Las Vegas?

Yes. Lee Canyon on Mount Charleston is about 45 minutes northwest of Las Vegas, with a base at 8,660 feet and 445 acres of terrain. This season opens Nov. 20, and lift tickets are sold online only, starting at $9 on the lowest-demand dates.

When does ski season start in Nevada?

It depends on snow. Mt. Rose opened Nov. 6 this season, Lee Canyon and Heavenly planned Nov. 20 openings, and Diamond Peak projects Dec. 3. Most Nevada resorts run until around mid-April, while the Elko SnoBowl runs weekends from January to mid-March.

How much is a lift ticket at Mt. Rose?

Mt. Rose uses dynamic pricing. Full window prices are $189 for adults and $95 for kids ages 6 to 15, and tickets for kids 5 and under are $20. Buying online by midnight the day before is cheaper, and half-day tickets are also available.

Do I need chains to drive to Mt. Rose or Heavenly?

During chain controls on the Mt. Rose Highway, Kingsbury Grade and U.S. 50 near Lake Tahoe, any vehicle without chains must be four-wheel or all-wheel drive with snow tires. Check nvroads.com or call 511 before you go, and carry chains to be safe.
