Christmas in Reno: Lights, Events, Holiday Trains and Ice Skating

Free lights downtown, a 100-foot light-show tree at the ballpark, holiday trains in Carson City and Virginia City, and what to know about December weather and roads.

Christmas in Reno runs from Thanksgiving weekend to Christmas Eve, with a free lights festival downtown, a 100-foot light-show tree at the Reno Aces ballpark and four holiday train rides within an hour's drive. Add cold, clear nights, a chance of snow and casinos that never close, and the Truckee Meadows makes a good December trip or a fun first holiday season for new residents. This guide covers the lights, events, trains, ice skating, shows and weather, with this year's dates where organizers have published them.

Quick answer

  • Kickoff: the free Holiday Lights Festival and city tree lighting downtown, Nov. 27 and 28, 4 to 9 p.m.
  • Biggest light show: Home for the Holidays at Greater Nevada Field, 19 nights from Nov. 27 to Dec. 23, 5 to 9 p.m. (ticketed).
  • Holiday trains: The Polar Express from Carson City (from $60) and the V&T's Candy Cane Express and Train O' Lights in Virginia City.
  • Classic show: The Nutcracker with A.V.A. Ballet Theatre and the Reno Phil at the Pioneer Center, Dec. 11 to 13.
  • Weather: cold. Expect freezing nights, possible snow, and chain controls on mountain roads to Lake Tahoe.

When does Christmas season start in Reno?

Reno's holiday season starts on Thanksgiving weekend and peaks in the first three weeks of December. This year the downtown Holiday Lights Festival and Home for the Holidays at the ballpark both open on Friday, Nov. 27, the day after Thanksgiving. The V&T Railway's Polar Express starts even earlier, in mid-November, and most holiday trains, shows and Santa visits wrap up by Dec. 20 to 23.

The first two weekends of December are the busiest: Sparks usually holds its Hometowne Christmas tree lighting and parade then, Carson City lights the Capitol tree, and the train rides sell out first. If you are flexible, weeknights in early December are the easiest time to see the lights without crowds.

Where are the best Christmas lights in Reno?

The best Christmas lights in Reno are the 100-foot tree at Greater Nevada Field, the free festival around City Plaza, and the decorated homes of the Hidden Valley neighborhood. Two of the three are free.

Home for the Holidays at Greater Nevada Field

The Reno Aces turn their downtown ballpark into a holiday light show for the second year. The centerpiece is a 100-foot Christmas tree on the field with light shows set to holiday music, with a new show every 20 minutes and lights on the stadium seats that play along. Families can take photos with Mr. and Mrs. Claus, write letters to Santa and make ornaments, and every ticket comes with a free hot chocolate, according to the Reno Aces event page.

This season runs 19 nights: Nov. 27 to 29, Dec. 3 to 6, Dec. 10 to 13 and Dec. 16 to 23, from 5 to 9 p.m. each night (Visit Reno Tahoe). Children 2 and under do not need a ticket. The Aces had not posted general admission prices on the event page when we checked, so look at the ticket link before you go; group igloos and suites are also available.

Holiday Lights Festival and tree lighting downtown

Reno's free kickoff takes place Friday and Saturday, Nov. 27 and 28, from 4 to 9 p.m., centered on City Plaza (home of the BELIEVE sculpture) and the Virginia Street Bridge over the Truckee River. Friday night includes the City of Reno's annual Christmas tree lighting, and Saturday adds a holiday car decorating event, according to KTVN 2 News. Admission and most activities are free, including photos with Santa, train rides, holiday movies and live entertainment; food, drinks and gifts from vendors cost extra (Visit Reno Tahoe).

Hidden Valley Parade of Lights

For a classic neighborhood drive, head to Hidden Valley in southeast Reno, where homeowners have decorated for more than four decades. Last season was the 43rd annual Hidden Valley Parade of Lights, running from Dec. 1 through New Year's Day and organized by the Hidden Valley Community Alliance. Streets that usually have the most decorated homes include Hidden Valley Drive, Pebble Beach Drive, Clear Creek Drive, Tamarisk Drive, Jagged Rock Road and Mia Vista Court, according to the community-maintained list at NewToReno. Reach the neighborhood from East McCarran Boulevard or Veterans Parkway via Pembroke Drive or Mira Loma Drive.

These are private homes on residential streets, so drive slowly, keep headlights low when stopped, and park only where it is legal. The same NewToReno page lists other decorated houses across Reno and Sparks, but notes that some entries are from past years.

What Christmas events are in Reno and Sparks?

Beyond the light shows, the main Reno Christmas events are the Sparks Hometowne Christmas parade, The Nutcracker, and a run of holiday concerts and library events.

Sparks Hometowne Christmas

Sparks celebrates in Victorian Square with a tree lighting on a Friday evening and the Hometowne Christmas Parade the next day. Last year the parade stepped off at 1 p.m. on Saturday, Dec. 6, running from Pyramid Way down Victorian Avenue, and it was free to watch, according to the Reno Tahoe Carson event listing. This year's dates had not been posted when we checked; the parade is traditionally on the first Saturday in December, so watch the City of Sparks site for the official schedule.

The Nutcracker at the Pioneer Center

A.V.A. Ballet Theatre and the Reno Phil perform The Nutcracker with a live orchestra at the Pioneer Center for the Performing Arts, 100 S. Virginia St. This year's shows are Friday, Dec. 11 at 8 p.m., and Saturday and Sunday, Dec. 12 and 13, at 1 and 7 p.m. Tickets run $33 to $72, with discounts for seniors and children, and a Sugar Plum party follows the weekend matinees, according to A.V.A. Ballet Theatre. The Pioneer Center box office is at 775-434-1050.

Concerts, Santa visits and free library events

December also brings holiday concerts, casino Christmas shows, and free crafts and Santa visits at Washoe County libraries. Check the Visit Reno Tahoe holiday events page and the venues' own calendars the week you plan to go.

Which Christmas train rides are near Reno?

Northern Nevada has four holiday train rides within about an hour of Reno: The Polar Express and the Santa Train in Carson City, and the Candy Cane Express and Train O' Lights in Virginia City. All of them sell out on December weekends, so book early.

The Polar Express from Carson City

The V&T Railway runs THE POLAR EXPRESS Train Ride from its Eastgate Depot in Carson City, about 35 minutes south of Reno. The one-hour round trip to "the North Pole" includes a staged performance of the story with costumed actors, music and narration, hot cocoa and treats delivered to your seat, and a silver bell for each child from Santa (V&T Railway). This year it runs from Nov. 13 to Dec. 23, with some exceptions, and tickets start at $60, according to Travel Nevada. Everyone 2 and older needs a ticket.

Candy Cane Express and Train O' Lights in Virginia City

Up the hill in Virginia City, the V&T runs two shorter holiday trains from its historic depot. The Very Merry Candy Cane Express runs three times a day on weekends from late November to Dec. 20, with cookies and hot chocolate or apple cider, for $32 to $38. The Electric Holiday Train O' Lights runs three times each Friday and Saturday evening from Nov. 27 to Dec. 19, ending at Santa's Workshop, for $42 to $46 (Travel Nevada). Combine a ride with an afternoon on C Street, where the old mining town decorates its boardwalks for the season.

Santa Train at the Nevada State Railroad Museum

The cheapest option is the Santa Train at the Nevada State Railroad Museum in Carson City: a 20-minute ride behind the restored steam locomotive No. 25 and the 1875 coach Glenbrook. It runs the first three weekends of December, Dec. 5 and 6, 12 and 13, and 19 and 20, with departures every 30 minutes. Museum admission is $10 for adults and free for ages 17 and under (Travel Nevada).

If you are heading east, the Nevada Northern Railway in Ely runs a 90-minute Santa's Reindeer Flyer on select dates from Nov. 21 to Dec. 26 ($45 adults, $38 seniors, $20 ages 4 to 12, free under 4).

Where can you go ice skating in Reno?

Reno has two places to skate in winter: the seasonal outdoor rink at the Grand Sierra Resort and the indoor, year-round Reno Ice arena.

The Ice Rink at Grand Sierra Resort (2500 E. Second St.) is an outdoor rink on the south side of the resort near the pool. Last season it opened Nov. 20, with doors at 4 p.m. daily, one-hour sessions starting at 5 p.m. and running until 9:30 p.m., and late sessions until midnight on Fridays and Saturdays. A session with skate rental cost $19, or $12 with your own skates, according to KOLO 8. Check the resort's site for this season's opening date and prices.

Reno Ice, the Jennifer M. O'Neal Community Ice Arena at 15500 Wedge Parkway in south Reno, is indoors, so it is the better choice on stormy nights. Public sessions last an hour and require booking online; admission has been $12 with $5 skate rental, per the NewToReno rink guide. Lake Tahoe adds more outdoor rinks at resort villages in winter, including in Truckee, Tahoe City and Northstar.

Is it worth a day trip to Carson City, Virginia City or Lake Tahoe?

Yes: Carson City, Virginia City and Lake Tahoe are all within about an hour of Reno and each adds something Reno doesn't have.

Carson City lights the tree at the Nevada State Capitol at its Silver & Snowflake Festival of Lights, listed for Friday, Dec. 4, from 5:30 to 8:30 p.m. It is free, with caroling, Santa, hot chocolate and free sleigh rides down Carson Street, according to Visit Carson City. Stay the night and ride the museum's Santa Train the next morning.

Virginia City, in Storey County, dresses up its 1870s main street for the season, and its saloons, candy shops and the V&T trains make an easy half-day trip. The drive climbs to about 6,200 feet on State Route 341, so it can be snowy when Reno is dry.

Lake Tahoe is the place for snow. The Mount Rose Highway climbs from Reno to Incline Village on the North Shore, passing Mt. Rose ski area on the way, and the Tahoe resort villages decorate for the holidays. Read our Reno-Tahoe region guide for more ideas.

What is the weather like in Reno in December?

December in Reno is cold and mostly dry: days are cool, nights usually drop below freezing, and a storm can bring snow to the valley. Reno sits at about 4,500 feet in the high desert, so it is far colder than Las Vegas, and the sun sets before 4:45 p.m. in mid-December. Winters vary a lot from year to year: last December was Reno's warmest on record, with an average temperature of 43°F, according to the National Weather Service in Reno.

Snow in Reno itself usually melts within a day or two, but the Sierra above town can get feet of it in a single storm. Before driving to Tahoe, Virginia City or over Interstate 80 to California, check chain controls and closures on NVRoads and carry chains, water, a blanket and a charged phone. For outdoor events, dress for temperatures in the 20s and 30s after dark: warm boots, gloves and a hat make the ballpark show and parade much more fun.

What is open in Reno on Christmas Day?

Reno's casino resorts stay open on Christmas Day, so restaurants, bars, buffets and some entertainment are available even when most other businesses close. Many casino restaurants offer holiday menus or buffets on Christmas Eve and Christmas Day; book ahead, because locals fill them too. Most holiday light shows, trains and Santa events end on Dec. 23 or earlier, so plan those for the days before Christmas. Neighborhood lights such as Hidden Valley stay on through the week after Christmas, and outdoor skating at the Grand Sierra continues into the new year.

Tips for Christmas in Reno

  • Book trains first. The Polar Express, Train O' Lights and Candy Cane Express sell out on December weekends.
  • Skip Saturdays if you can. Home for the Holidays is closed most Mondays to Wednesdays until its final week, so Thursday nights are usually the least crowded time to go.
  • Park once downtown. On festival nights, park in a casino garage or a paid lot and walk to City Plaza and the ballpark, which are about half a mile apart.
  • Layer up. Outdoor events happen after sunset, when it is often below freezing.
  • Check the roads. Plan mountain drives for daylight and check NVRoads before you leave.

Christmas in Reno: FAQ

Is Reno a good place to spend Christmas?

Yes, if you like a small-city holiday with real winter weather. Reno has a free downtown lights festival, a big light show at the ballpark, The Nutcracker, holiday trains nearby and casinos that stay open on Christmas Day, plus Lake Tahoe snow within an hour.

When is the Reno Christmas tree lighting?

The City of Reno lights its tree on the first night of the Holiday Lights Festival, which this year is Friday, Nov. 27, at City Plaza downtown. The festival runs 4 to 9 p.m. on Nov. 27 and 28 and is free.

Where can I see Christmas lights in Reno for free?

The Holiday Lights Festival at City Plaza and the Virginia Street Bridge is free, and the Hidden Valley Parade of Lights in southeast Reno is a free drive-by display of decorated homes that usually runs from Dec. 1 to New Year's Day.

Does the Polar Express leave from Virginia City or Carson City?

The Polar Express departs from the V&T Railway's Eastgate Depot in Carson City, about 35 minutes south of Reno. The V&T's Candy Cane Express and Train O' Lights are the holiday trains that run in Virginia City.

Does it snow in Reno at Christmas?

Sometimes. Reno is in the high desert and many Decembers are dry, but storms can bring snow to the valley. A white Christmas is far more likely at Lake Tahoe, Mount Rose and Virginia City, which sit roughly 1,500 to 4,000 feet higher.

What is there to do in Reno on Christmas Day?

Casino resorts stay open, so you can eat at a holiday buffet or restaurant, see a show, or skate at the Grand Sierra Resort's outdoor rink. Most light shows and trains end by Dec. 23, but neighborhood lights stay on through New Year's.

If you are deciding between northern and southern Nevada for the holidays, compare this with Christmas in Las Vegas, where December days are much milder.
