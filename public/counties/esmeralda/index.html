About Esmeralda County

Esmeralda County is in Nevada's Central Nevada region.

Esmeralda County is a county in the southwestern portion of the U.S. state of Nevada. As of the 2025 estimate, the population was 729, making it the least populous county in Nevada, and the 20th least populous county in the United States. Esmeralda County does not have any incorporated communities. Its county seat is the town of Goldfield.

Its 2000 census population density of 0.2706 inhabitants per square mile was the second-lowest of any county in the contiguous United States (above Loving County, Texas). Its school district does not have a high school, so students in grades 9–12 go to school in Tonopah, in the Nye County School District.

Most residents live in Goldfield or in the town of Dyer in Fish Lake Valley, near the California border.

Esmeralda County: common questions

What is the population of Esmeralda County, Nevada?

Esmeralda County had 729 residents at the 2020 Census, the 17th most of Nevada's 17 counties. The 2024 estimate is 720.

What is the county seat of Esmeralda County?

The county seat is Goldfield.

How big is Esmeralda County?

Esmeralda County covers about 3,581 square miles of land, the 13th largest of Nevada's counties.

What cities and towns are in Esmeralda County?

Esmeralda County includes Dyer, Goldfield, Silver Peak.

What school district serves Esmeralda County?

The Esmeralda County School District runs public schools across the whole county.

How many people in Esmeralda County live in a city or town?

About 79% of residents live inside a city or census-designated town; the rest live in rural areas outside them.
