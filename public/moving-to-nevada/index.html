Moving to Nevada

Deadlines, taxes, utilities and how to pick a town

Nevada draws newcomers for its lack of a state income tax, its job markets in Las Vegas and Reno, and the open land in between. This guide covers the deadlines and costs that catch people out, and how to pick a town.

First 30 days checklist

  1. Driver's license: get a Nevada license within 30 days of becoming a resident (DMV new resident guide). Book an appointment early; urban DMV offices are busy.
  2. Vehicle registration: register your vehicles within the same 30 days. You will need Nevada insurance, and in the Las Vegas and Reno areas most vehicles need an emissions (smog) test first (registration requirements).
  3. Voter registration: update it online once you have your Nevada license (registertovotenv.gov). Active voters are mailed a ballot for each election unless they opt out.
  4. Utilities: set up electricity, gas, water and trash before move-in day; see below for who provides what.
  5. Schools: enroll children with the county school district, which assigns schools by address.

Taxes and costs

No state income tax. Nevada does not tax wages, retirement income or Social Security. The state relies instead on sales tax and taxes on gaming and tourism.

Sales tax is at least 6.85% everywhere in the state and reaches 8.375% in Clark County, which includes Las Vegas and Henderson.

Property tax is limited by a partial abatement: for an owner-occupied primary home, the tax bill generally cannot rise more than 3% a year, regardless of how fast the assessed value climbs. Rental and second homes have a higher cap.

Vehicle registration includes the Governmental Services Tax, based on the vehicle's original price and age, so the first registration of a newer car can cost more than newcomers expect.

Check rates with the Nevada Department of Taxation and your county assessor before you buy.

Choosing a town

The Las Vegas Valley has most of the state's jobs and housing. Henderson and Summerlin South (west of the city) are popular with families; Paradise and Winchester are close to the Strip; North Las Vegas and Enterprise have much of the newer construction. See the Las Vegas & Southern Nevada region.

Reno and Sparks offer four seasons and quick access to Lake Tahoe, with a job base in logistics, manufacturing and the university. Nearby Fernley, Dayton and Spanish Springs are lower-cost commuter options. See Reno-Tahoe.

Carson City and the Carson Valley (Minden, Gardnerville) are quieter, with government and health-care jobs. Rural Nevada towns such as Elko, Winnemucca, Fallon and Ely have lower housing costs and mining or military jobs, but long drives to specialists and big-box shopping.

Climate

Southern Nevada is hot desert: summer highs above 100°F are normal in Las Vegas, winters are mild, and rain is rare. Northern Nevada is high desert, at 4,000 feet and above, with cold winters, snow in the mountains, big day-night temperature swings and dry, warm summers. Wildfire smoke can affect air quality in summer statewide; check the AirNow fire and smoke map.

Utilities

NV Energy provides electricity to most of the state, including Las Vegas, Reno and Carson City. Some areas are served by local cooperatives or districts instead, such as Valley Electric Association around Pahrump, Overton Power District No. 5 in the Moapa Valley and Mesquite, Mt. Wheeler Power around Ely and Wells Rural Electric in parts of Elko County. Natural gas comes from Southwest Gas in most of the state and NV Energy in the Reno area; many rural homes use propane. Each city and town page lists the providers for that area.

Southern Nevada gets about 90 percent of its water from the Colorado River through Lake Mead, and outdoor watering is restricted by season. Ask your water provider about landscaping rules before you plant a lawn.

Schools

Nevada has one public school district per county, so the Clark County School District serves all of the Las Vegas Valley and the Washoe County School District serves Reno, Sparks and Incline Village. Charter schools, many sponsored by the State Public Charter School Authority, are another option. Search schools by address with the NCES school search or the Nevada Department of Education.

Moving to Nevada: common questions

Does Nevada have a state income tax?

No. Nevada does not tax personal income, including wages, pensions and Social Security benefits.

How long do I have to get a Nevada driver's license after moving?

New residents must get a Nevada driver's license and register their vehicles within 30 days of moving to the state.

What is the sales tax in Nevada?

The statewide minimum is 6.85%, and counties can add more. Clark County's combined rate is 8.375%, the highest in the state.

Do I need a smog check to register a car in Nevada?

Most gasoline and diesel vehicles registered in the urban parts of Clark and Washoe counties need an emissions test. Rural counties do not require one.

Can I register to vote online in Nevada?

Yes, with a current Nevada driver's license or ID card. Nevada also offers same-day registration in person during early voting and on Election Day.

Is Las Vegas or Reno cheaper to live in?

It depends on the neighborhood and the year, so compare current listings. Both are much more expensive than rural towns such as Elko, Fallon or Winnemucca, where housing is the biggest savings.
