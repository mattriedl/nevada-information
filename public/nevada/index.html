Overview

Nevada sits almost entirely within the Great Basin, a region where rivers drain inward rather than to the sea, and the state is crossed by more than 300 north-south mountain ranges separated by flat valleys. The far south is Mojave Desert, home to Las Vegas, and the far west climbs into the Sierra Nevada at Lake Tahoe.

Mining built the state, starting with the Comstock Lode in 1859, and gold mining remains a major industry in northern Nevada. Legalized casino gambling in 1931 set Las Vegas and Reno on course to become resort cities.

People

Nevada is one of the most urban states in the country despite its empty map: Clark County holds about 73% of residents, and Washoe County, centered on Reno, holds about 16%. The other 15 county areas share the remaining population across most of the state's land. See the full list of cities and towns.

Land

About 80 percent of Nevada is managed by federal agencies, chiefly the Bureau of Land Management, along with national forests, military ranges and national parks. That public land is why so much of the state is open for hiking, off-roading, hunting and camping, and also why towns rarely sprawl beyond their valleys.

Nevada facts: common questions

When did Nevada become a state?

Nevada was admitted to the Union on October 31, 1864, during the Civil War, which is why its state flag and nickname say "Battle Born." Nevada Day is still a state holiday.

What is the capital of Nevada?

Carson City is the capital. Las Vegas is by far the largest city, and Reno is the largest in northern Nevada.

How many people live in Nevada?

Nevada had 3,104,614 residents at the 2020 Census. The Census Bureau estimated 3,267,467 in mid-2024. About 73% live in Clark County and 16% in Washoe County.

What time zone is Nevada in?

Nevada is on Pacific Time. The exception is West Wendover on the Utah line, which observes Mountain Time, as does the border town of Jackpot by local practice.

Why is Nevada called the Silver State?

The Comstock Lode silver strike near Virginia City in 1859 set off a mining rush that led to statehood five years later.
