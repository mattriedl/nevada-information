Visiting Nevada

Beyond the Strip: parks, lakes and the long roads between them

On this page

  1. Where to go
  2. When to go
  3. Driving
  4. FAQ

Most visitors see Las Vegas and Lake Tahoe. The rest of Nevada rewards a road trip: red-rock parks, alpine lakes, mining towns and national parks, separated by long empty highways.

Where to go

  • Red Rock Canyon National Conservation AreaSandstone cliffs and a 13-mile scenic drive about 20 minutes west of the Strip. Timed entry reservations are required for the drive in the busy season. Nearest town: Las Vegas.
  • Valley of Fire State ParkNevada's oldest state park, with red Aztec sandstone, petroglyphs and the Fire Wave hike, about an hour northeast of Las Vegas. Nearest town: Moapa Valley.
  • Hoover Dam and Lake MeadTour the dam, then swim, boat or walk the Historic Railroad Trail in the national recreation area. Nearest town: Boulder City.
  • Lake Tahoe's Nevada shoreSand Harbor's clear water and boulders, the Tahoe East Shore Trail and ski areas above Incline Village and Stateline. Nearest town: Incline Village.
  • Virginia CityThe Comstock boomtown, with wooden boardwalks, saloons, mine tours and steam train rides, 35 minutes from Reno. Nearest town: Virginia City.
  • Great Basin National ParkWheeler Peak, Lehman Caves tours and 4,000-year-old bristlecone pines on the Utah line. Some of the darkest night skies in the country. Nearest town: Ely.
  • Ruby Mountains and Lamoille CanyonAn alpine canyon of glacier-carved walls and lakes above Elko, often called the Swiss Alps of Nevada. Nearest town: Elko.
  • Pyramid LakeA desert lake on the Pyramid Lake Paiute Reservation, 40 minutes north of Reno; buy a tribal permit before fishing or camping. Nearest town: Reno.
  • US 50, "The Loneliest Road"Fallon to Ely by way of Austin and Eureka: hundreds of miles of basins, ranges and historic mining towns. Nearest town: Fallon.

When to go

Spring and fall are the best seasons for hiking in southern Nevada; summer highs in Las Vegas regularly pass 100°F. Northern Nevada and Lake Tahoe are at their best in summer and early fall, and the Reno-Tahoe area becomes a ski destination from December into spring.

Driving tips

  • Check NVRoads or dial 511 before mountain drives, especially in winter.
  • Fill up whenever you can on rural highways; stretches of US 50, US 6 and US 95 run 100 miles or more without services.
  • Carry extra water, and do not rely on phone navigation alone in areas with no cell signal.
  • Watch for open-range cattle and wildlife at night. Find more ideas at Travel Nevada and Nevada State Parks.

Visiting Nevada: common questions

When is the best time to visit Nevada?

Spring (March to May) and fall (September to November) are best for most of the state. Summer is ideal for Lake Tahoe and the high country but very hot in southern Nevada, and winter brings skiing near Reno and snow-closed roads in the mountains.

How do I check road conditions in Nevada?

Use NVRoads.com or dial 511 for closures, chain controls and construction from the Nevada Department of Transportation.

Do I need a reservation for Red Rock Canyon?

Timed-entry reservations are required for the Red Rock Canyon Scenic Drive during the busy season. Check the Bureau of Land Management's current rules before you go.

Is it safe to drive across rural Nevada?

Yes, with preparation. Gas stations can be 100 miles or more apart, cell service drops out between towns, and summer heat is dangerous, so fill up often and carry water.
